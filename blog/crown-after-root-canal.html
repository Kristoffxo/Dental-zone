Root Canal

Is a crown necessary after a root canal?

It is the question we are asked most often after treatment, usually with a suspicion that we are upselling. We are not. Here is the actual reason.

2026-08-19·2 min read

What a root canal does to the tooth

A root canal removes the pulp — the nerve and blood supply — from inside the tooth. That is what stops the pain and clears the infection. But it also means the tooth no longer receives moisture and nutrients from inside.

Over the following months the tooth gradually becomes more brittle. It looks identical. It feels identical. It is measurably weaker.

Why back teeth need a crown

Your molars take the overwhelming majority of chewing force — several times what a front tooth handles. A brittle molar under that load does not develop a small crack you can catch early. It splits, often vertically, straight down through the root.

A vertically fractured root cannot be repaired. The tooth has to come out, and you are then choosing between an implant, a bridge, or living with a gap. That is the outcome a crown is protecting you from.

When you may not need one

We will tell you if you do not. Front teeth — incisors and canines — bite rather than grind, and take far less force. If the tooth is otherwise largely intact, a well-bonded filling is often enough.

The deciding factor is usually how much natural tooth structure is left. A tooth that needed a large cavity cleared before treatment has little of itself remaining, and that tooth needs a crown regardless of where it sits.

How long you can leave it

Not indefinitely. The usual advice is a few weeks to a couple of months after the root canal settles. We have seen patients return two years later with a tooth that split while eating something entirely ordinary — and by then the root canal they paid for has been wasted along with the tooth.

What it involves

Two visits in most cases. The tooth is shaped, an impression is taken, and a temporary crown protects it while the permanent one is made. At the second visit the permanent crown is fitted and cemented. Neither appointment is uncomfortable — the nerve is already gone.

Common questions

Can I skip the crown to save money?

You can, and we will not refuse to treat you. But if the tooth splits — and for a molar that is likely rather than possible — you lose the tooth and the money already spent on the root canal. An extraction plus an implant costs considerably more than the crown would have.

How long does a dental crown last?

With good hygiene and regular checkups, commonly ten to fifteen years, often longer. What ends a crown's life is usually decay at the margin where it meets the tooth, not the crown itself failing.
