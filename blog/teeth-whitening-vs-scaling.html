Teeth whitening vs scaling: which one do you actually need?

Patients frequently ask us for whitening when what they describe is a job for a cleaning. They are different procedures solving different problems, and the cheaper one is often the right one.

2026-08-19·2 min read

The difference in one line

Scaling removes what is stuck on your teeth. Whitening changes the colour of the teeth themselves.

If your teeth have gone yellow-brown from tea, coffee, tobacco or hardened tartar, that colour is sitting on the surface. Scaling and polishing removes it, and people are regularly surprised at how much brighter their teeth are afterwards — no whitening required.

If the tooth is uniformly darker in its own structure, no amount of cleaning changes it. That is what whitening is for.

Start with a cleaning

We will nearly always suggest scaling first, for two practical reasons. It may be all you need. And if you do go on to whiten, the result is far more even on a clean surface — bleaching over a layer of tartar produces a patchy outcome.

The whitening options

In-clinic whitening

A high-concentration gel applied under controlled conditions, with the gums protected. Around an hour, and the change is visible the same day. The fastest route, and the one where the concentration is high enough that it genuinely needs supervising.

Take-home trays

Custom trays made from impressions of your teeth, with a lower-strength gel worn for a set period daily over two to three weeks. Slower, gentler on sensitivity, and generally less expensive.

Whitening toothpaste

Mildly abrasive, so it removes some surface staining. It does not change the colour of the tooth. Reasonable for maintaining a result; ineffective at creating one.

What whitening will not do

  • It does not work on crowns, veneers or fillings. These keep their original shade. If you have a white filling on a front tooth and whiten around it, it will suddenly stand out — which is why we plan the order carefully.
  • It does not fix greyish discolouration from an old root canal. That needs a different approach, usually internal bleaching or a veneer.
  • It is not permanent. Expect one to three years depending on tea, coffee and tobacco.

Sensitivity

Common, and usually temporary. Most people notice a few days of sharper reaction to cold, which settles. If you already have sensitive teeth, tell us — we would generally choose the slower take-home route for you, which is markedly gentler.

A word on shop-bought kits

The strips and kits sold online vary enormously in quality, and the risk is not the whitening — it is the gel sitting against the gums in an ill-fitting tray, which causes chemical burns we then have to treat. If cost is the concern, tell us and we will talk about the take-home option honestly.

Common questions

Is teeth whitening safe?

Done professionally, yes. The gel is applied with the gums protected and the concentration is matched to your teeth. The problems we see come almost entirely from unsupervised shop-bought kits with poorly fitting trays, where gel contacts the gum tissue.

How long does teeth whitening last?

Typically one to three years. It depends heavily on tea, coffee, red wine and tobacco. A cleaning every six months and cutting back on staining drinks extends it considerably.
