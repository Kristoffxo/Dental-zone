Are dental implants safe if you have diabetes?

Patients often assume diabetes rules them out entirely. It does not. What matters is not whether you have it, but how well controlled it is.

2026-08-19·2 min read

The short answer

If your diabetes is well controlled, implant success rates are close to those of people without diabetes. If it is poorly controlled, the risks rise meaningfully. The diagnosis is not the deciding factor — the control is.

This is one of the most common reasons people never ask about implants, and it costs them years of managing with a gap or a loose denture unnecessarily.

Why control matters so much

An implant works by fusing with the jawbone over several months. That process depends on healing, and persistently high blood glucose interferes with healing in three specific ways: it slows new bone formation, it impairs the white cells that fight infection, and it reduces blood supply to small vessels.

Each of those matters most in the first few months, which is exactly the window when the implant is integrating.

What we look at

We will ask for a recent HbA1c — the three-month average of your blood glucose, which is far more useful to us than a single fasting reading taken on a good morning.

  • Well controlled: generally treated much like any other patient, with normal healing expectations.
  • Moderately controlled: usually workable, often with a longer healing period before the crown is fitted, and closer follow-up.
  • Poorly controlled: we would advise working with your physician to improve control first. This is not a refusal — it is avoiding an implant that fails and money that is wasted.

Gum disease first

Diabetes and gum disease worsen each other, and many patients with long-standing diabetes have some degree of it. Any active gum disease must be treated before an implant goes in, or the same process attacks the tissue around the implant — a condition called peri-implantitis, which is the most common reason implants are lost late.

Practical things that help

  • Morning appointments, after your normal breakfast and medication, so your glucose is stable.
  • Tell us every medication you take, including anything for blood pressure or cholesterol.
  • Expect a longer healing window. We would rather wait an extra month before loading the implant than rush it.
  • Keep your follow-ups. With diabetes we like to see you more often in the first year, not because something is wrong but because catching a problem early is straightforward and catching it late often is not.

If you also smoke or chew tobacco

Say so. Tobacco and diabetes together raise implant failure risk far more than either alone, because both impair exactly the same healing process. We will still treat you, and we will be honest with you about the odds.

Common questions

What HbA1c is acceptable for a dental implant?

There is no single universal cut-off, and it is a judgement made with your physician rather than a number applied mechanically. Broadly, the better controlled you are the closer your success rate is to a non-diabetic patient. We will ask for a recent reading and discuss it with you honestly.

Will my implant fail because I have diabetes?

Not necessarily. Well-controlled diabetes carries a success rate close to that of patients without diabetes. Poor control, especially combined with smoking or untreated gum disease, is where the risk rises significantly.
