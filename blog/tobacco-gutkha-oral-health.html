What gutkha and tobacco actually do to your mouth

This is the one we most wish people read early. In our chairs in Lukarganj, chewing tobacco causes more serious disease than anything else — and the early signs are painless.

2026-08-19·2 min read

Why chewing is worse than smoking, for the mouth

Smoke passes through. A packet of gutkha or khaini sits in one spot against the cheek or gum, often for long stretches, several times a day, for years. That tissue is in continuous contact with the irritants.

This is why the damage is so often one-sided. Patients tell us the problem is on the side they habitually park it — and they are usually right.

The warning signs, in order of seriousness

  • A white or grey patch that does not rub off. This is leukoplakia. It is painless, which is exactly why it gets ignored.
  • A red patch. Less common than white, and more concerning.
  • Your mouth not opening as wide as it used to. This is often submucous fibrosis — the tissue stiffening. Many people notice it first when eating a large roti becomes awkward.
  • A burning feeling with spicy food that was not there before.
  • An ulcer that has not healed in two weeks. Ordinary mouth ulcers heal in about ten days. One that does not needs looking at, not waiting on.

The thing people get wrong

Almost everyone assumes pain is the warning sign. In the mouth it is usually the last one to arrive. Leukoplakia does not hurt. Early submucous fibrosis does not hurt. By the stage something is genuinely painful, the problem has usually been developing for a long time.

If you use tobacco in any form, get your mouth examined every six months regardless of how it feels. The examination takes a few minutes and involves nothing more than looking and feeling.

What we can do

A great deal, if you come early. Patches can be monitored, biopsied where needed, and removed. Fibrosis can be managed and sometimes improved, particularly if you stop. Teeth stained by years of use can be cleaned back to a normal colour, and the gum damage underneath treated.

What we cannot do is undo advanced disease. That is the entire argument for coming in while nothing hurts.

If you are trying to stop

Tell us. It changes what we look for and how often we want to see you. We are not here to lecture — most people who chew already know the risks, and being told again rarely helps. What does help is somebody checking your mouth properly twice a year.

Common questions

I only have one or two packets a day. Is that a risk?

Yes. Risk rises with quantity and years, but there is no amount considered safe, and we regularly see changes in people who describe their use as light. Duration matters as much as quantity.

How do I know if a mouth patch is serious?

You cannot tell by looking, and neither can we with certainty — that is what a biopsy is for. The practical rule is simple: any patch or ulcer that has not gone in two weeks should be examined.
